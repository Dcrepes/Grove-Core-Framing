Anchors & Strapping · Lennar

What holds your home together

Behind the drywall, every Lennar home is tied together with steel connectors. They lock the roof to the walls and the walls to the concrete slab, so the house resists Florida wind as one piece. Here are the six parts we rely on most and what each one does.

The load path

One connected chain, roof to slab

When wind pushes on a house, it tries to lift the roof and slide the walls. Each connector passes that force down to the next piece until it reaches the concrete, which holds it.

  • 1Titen HD anchor: wall to slab
  • 2Wedge anchor: heavy hardware to slab
  • 3HTT4 hold-down: wall corners to slab
  • 44½" SDWC screw: studs to plates
  • 5MSTA24 strap: framing pieces to each other
  • 66" SDWC screw: roof to walls
1

Titen HD screw anchor

THD50600H · ½" × 6"

A heavy-duty screw made for concrete. The installer drills a hole in the slab and drives this anchor straight in; its hardened threads cut into the concrete and grip it tight. This is the anchor we use most, often 70 to 100 in a single home.

Where it goes
Through the bottom board of each wall, down into the slab
Why it matters
Keeps walls from lifting or sliding off the foundation in high wind
2

Wedge anchor

WA62120 · ⅝" × 12"

A thick steel bolt for heavier connections. It goes into a drilled hole in the concrete; when the nut on top is tightened, a steel clip at the bottom wedges outward against the hole and locks the anchor in place.

Where it goes
Into the slab, holding down hold-downs, posts and other heavy hardware
Why it matters
Provides a strong attachment point wherever a connector has to resist big upward forces
3

Tension tie (hold-down)

HTT4 · 12⅜"

A tall steel bracket that is nailed to the end post of a wall and then bolted to an anchor in the slab. It keeps the corners and ends of walls planted on the foundation when wind tries to tip the wall over.

Where it goes
At the ends of the walls that brace the house, connected to an anchor in the concrete
Why it matters
Stops walls from rocking up at one end under hurricane loads
4

Structural wood screw, 4½"

SDWC15450 · 50 pack

A long, slim structural screw driven at an angle through the wall framing. It fastens each wall stud to the boards that run along the top and bottom of the wall, and it is rated to hold against uplift.

Where it goes
Where the vertical studs meet the top and bottom plates of the wall
Why it matters
Keeps the wall frame from pulling apart, so wind forces keep traveling down to the slab
5

Strap tie

MSTA24 · 24"

A flat, galvanized steel strap with nail holes along its length. It is laid across a joint where two pieces of framing meet and nailed to both, so the two pieces act as one.

Where it goes
Across joints in the framing, such as wall plates, beams and headers over openings
Why it matters
Bridges seams in the framing so the load path never has a weak link
6

Truss screw, 6"

SDWC15600 · 50 pack

The longer version of the screw above. It is driven up through the top of the wall into each roof truss, tying the roof to the walls. Its flat cap head sits flush in the wood, so it stays hidden behind the ceiling.

Where it goes
Through the double top plate of the wall into every roof truss
Why it matters
Holds the roof down when hurricane winds try to lift it off the house

Connectors are manufactured by Simpson Strong-Tie and supplied through our framing lumber vendor. Drawings are simplified illustrations, not to scale with each other.

The exact type, count and placement of connectors in each home is set by the engineered plans and local building code.
